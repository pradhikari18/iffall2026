Chicken Momos

Momos are steamed dumplings and are one of Nepal's most traditional dishes. A thin dough wrapper is filled with a seasoned mixture of ground meat and vegetables, folded by hand, then steamed and served with a spicy tomato-based dipping sauce called achar.

Ingredients

  • Dough: 2 cups all-purpose flour
  • 3/4 cup water
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • Filling: 1 lb ground chicken
  • 1 small onion, finely chopped
  • 3 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1 tablespoon fresh ginger, minced
  • 1/4 cup cilantro, chopped
  • 1 teaspoon cumin powder
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 1 tablespoon oil

Instructions

  1. Mix flour, salt, and water into a smooth dough. Cover and rest for 20 minutes.
  2. Combine ground chicken, onion, garlic, ginger, cilantro, cumin, salt, and oil in a bowl to make the filling.
  3. Roll the dough into small, thin circles.
  4. Place a spoonful of filling in the center of each circle and pleat the edges closed.
  5. Arrange momos in a steamer lined with oil or cabbage leaves so they don't stick.
  6. Steam for 12-15 minutes, until the wrapper is translucent and the filling is cooked through.
  7. Serve hot with tomato achar (dipping sauce).

Recipe Website References

Cook With Manali

This site has a detailed momo recipe with step-by-step photos of the dough folding process, which is one of the trickiest parts to explain in text alone. It's a strong reference for how to pair instructions with visuals at each key step.

Serious Eats

Serious Eats uses a magazine-style layout with a clear ingredient list next to a large photo. Recipes are easy to scan, and the mix of short paragraphs with helpful step photos makes it a strong reference for readability.

NYT Cooking

NYT Cooking has a very minimal design with lots of white space and simple typography. It puts the food photography front and center, which is a good model for a design that feels calm rather than cluttered.

Non-Recipe Website References

Stripe

Stripe's site uses a strong grid and confident color palette. I like how sections are clearly separated without heavy borders.

Apple

Apple's product pages use large imagery paired with short text. This full-width image approach could work well for a hero photo of the finished dish before diving into ingredients and steps.

Headspace

Headspace mixes friendly illustration with approachable copy. Their use of warm colors and rounded shapes could inspire a more playful tone, fitting for a dish like momos that comes with a story and cultural background.
